# A human rhythm for software

Meridian is a design language for interfaces that feel clear, tangible and responsive. Natural materials offer useful cues: light reveals layers, a surface yields to contact, and an object stays recognisable as its position changes. We use those cues to help people understand an interface while keeping their content central.

## Familiarity is a hypothesis

Natural inspiration does not guarantee intuitive use. People bring different conventions, languages, abilities and devices. A material earns its place when people can find an action, understand its result and recover from mistakes. Preference and delight are worth studying alongside task success; neither substitutes for it.

## Light explains relationships

Light and shading communicate depth and containment. Use a consistent light direction within a composition. Reserve stronger relief for interactive instruments and temporary layers. Reading, writing, data and artwork need calm surfaces. Refraction belongs at peripheral edges; it must not distort text or the evidence people are judging.

## Respond, then settle

A control acknowledges contact immediately. Optional springs explain the transition after the action begins. The label and hit target remain stable even when the decorative surface yields. Focus, hover, press, selection and completion are different states. Animation never delays an action or announces a result that has not happened.

Reduced motion keeps the same information without spatial travel. Opaque materials, increased contrast and forced colors are designed appearances with equal functionality. They are not degraded versions of Meridian.

## Six environments, two appearances

Prism is the neutral primary environment: clarity, luminous surfaces and restrained reflection. Forest is green, organic and grounded. Desert uses yellow and gold, sand and sun-warmed space. Ocean brings blue depth, water clarity and cool mineral edges. Aurora uses purple and pink light with atmospheric crystal diffusion. Canyon draws from red sandstone, terracotta, copper and warm reflected light.

Dawn and Dusk are lighting conditions within each environment. Functional meanings remain consistent: an error is identifiable in Canyon; a successful action is explicit in Forest; focus remains visible in Aurora. No environment changes the meaning of a command.

## One language, deliberate adaptations

A keyboard, touchscreen, crown, controller and rotary knob have different strengths. Share action semantics, material character and state meaning. Adapt target size, focus navigation, density, reach and feedback to the input and setting. A foldable must preserve the task during posture changes. Spatial and driving interfaces require their own comfort and safety evaluation; a scaled browser preview cannot establish that support.

## Content leads

Keep essential navigation discoverable while allowing secondary instruments to recede. Returning focus or requesting an action must reveal the relevant controls. Never put the only route to an action behind hover, animation, an unlabelled gesture or a translucent surface that disappears into the content.

## Evidence before adoption

The web reference, component designs and Material Model lab serve different purposes. A visual specimen is not a working component package. Automated checks establish specific properties; human and assistive-technology evaluation establish others. Candidate, Preview and Lab labels stay explicit until the relevant review is complete.

Polaris coordinates decisions and evidence. Meridian owns interaction and accessibility intent. Axis supports architecture and implementation. Sentinel contributes security and trust-boundary review. Product-specific adoption belongs to each product and retains its own evidence.
