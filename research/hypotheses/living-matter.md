# Living Matter — hypothesis and evaluation registry

**Programme:** Meridian 0.6 (exploratory). **Evidence status:** All hypotheses below are **speculative/unvalidated for Meridian** at creation. No participant results, effect-size estimates or physiological claims are asserted.

## Common evaluation rules

- Compare experimental variants against functionally equivalent conventional interaction versions.
- Separate preference, enjoyment, recognition, task completion time and error rate; one does not substitute for another.
- Test representative input methods (keyboard, pointer and touch where meaningful) and accessibility preferences.
- Record user tasks, participants, equipment, browser versions, sequence and context; counterbalance presentation order where possible.
- Start with formative studies. Use controlled confirmatory tests and power/sample-size planning before claims of superior performance.
- Obtain participant consent, minimise recorded personal data and publish only appropriately anonymised results.

## Hypotheses

### LM-H01 — Attraction as discoverability

**Hypothesis:** A subtle proximity-responsive visual treatment may improve perceived discoverability of an actionable control without harming activation accuracy.  
**Independent variable:** Static hover/focus vs cosmetic magnetic attraction.  
**Measures:** Control recognition, misclicks, action completion, perceived ease, frustration.  
**Falsification concern:** Increased missed clicks or reduced clarity; effects that shift hit areas are disallowed regardless of preference.  
**Status:** Speculative.

### LM-H02 — Liquid grouping as relationship cue

**Hypothesis:** Liquid-style visual continuity may clarify transitions between related controls or selected states.  
**Independent variable:** Ordinary state transition vs merging/separation animation.  
**Measures:** Correct explanation of relationship, selection errors, time, subjective clarity.  
**Falsification concern:** Merged shapes obscure distinct targets, semantics or state.  
**Status:** Speculative.

### LM-H03 — Elastic contact as acknowledgement

**Hypothesis:** Compression/rebound may increase perceived immediacy or satisfaction when feedback is brief and the action is not delayed.  
**Independent variable:** Immediate colour/shape feedback vs controlled spring compression.  
**Measures:** Reported responsiveness, task time, error rate, preference, motion discomfort.  
**Falsification concern:** Rebound looks entertaining but reduces task throughput or produces discomfort.  
**Status:** Speculative.

### LM-H04 — Spatial continuity as orientation

**Hypothesis:** Spatially coherent transitions may improve users' understanding of navigation relationships.  
**Independent variable:** Instant view switch vs directional/spatial movement.  
**Measures:** Return-path accuracy, spatial recall, time, confusion, preference.  
**Falsification concern:** Parallax or inertial motion adds ambiguity or vestibular discomfort.  
**Status:** Speculative.

### LM-H05 — Viscosity as manipulation feedback

**Hypothesis:** A constrained viscous/morphing surface may clarify direct-manipulation boundaries in creative contexts.  
**Independent variable:** Immediate transform vs expressive deformation.  
**Measures:** Control accuracy, completed manipulation, time, confidence, predictability.  
**Falsification concern:** Motion competes with feedback, warps essential text or increases errors.  
**Status:** Speculative.

### LM-H06 — Glass as material hierarchy

**Hypothesis:** Protected translucency may increase perceived layer distinction or visual appeal, but may reduce legibility over complex backgrounds.  
**Independent variable:** Solid overlay vs controlled glass treatment, tested over simple and complex grounds.  
**Measures:** Reading accuracy, target identification, time, perceived depth, discomfort.  
**Falsification concern:** Any loss of essential legibility or focus visibility; use opaque fallback.  
**Status:** Speculative.

### LM-H07 — Environmental familiarity (cross-cutting)

**Hypothesis:** Natural environmental palettes/materials may influence approachability and preference differently across tasks and audiences.  
**Independent variable:** Product-identity control vs one or more environment treatments with semantic hierarchy held fixed.  
**Measures:** Semantic consistency, visual preference, reading/task outcomes, reported cultural familiarity.  
**Falsification concern:** Mistaking preference for universal comfort, or overwriting product brand meaning.  
**Status:** Speculative.

## Evidence and decision template

For each experiment, record:

- Hypothesis ID, version, investigator/reviewer and date.
- Prior sources with full citations and stated limitations.
- Intended product task and target user context.
- Tested versions, prototype URLs/commit, Figma node IDs.
- Device/input/accessibility settings and controlled variables.
- Consent and data-handling method for any user testing.
- Observations, errors, positive/negative results and contradictions.
- Scope of any supportable conclusions.
- Decision: reject / revise / retain in Lab / candidate for stable Design.
- Links to GitHub issue, PR, ADR and planned release.

**Evidence classification:** Speculative → Exploratory → Evidence-supported → Validated for scope. These are provenance/quality labels, not assertions of universal scientific proof.
